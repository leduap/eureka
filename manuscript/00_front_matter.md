# The Finance Blue Book

What the MBA finance course teaches, and what the case leaves out

Ashok Paudel

---

This is the manuscript for editing. The cases are original. They are written in the form used in the MBA finance classroom at places such as ESMT Berlin and Harvard Business School: a firm, a decision, a few numbers, and a question you are expected to answer before anyone explains it. They are not those schools' cases, and they are not a record of either course.

The arithmetic is teaching arithmetic. It is produced by `ledger/ledger.py`, which is the source of every euro in the book. The tax rate used in the running examples is 30 percent, so the sums can be checked with a calculator. A Munich company pays more than that. Chapter 10 shows the difference, and the sources are at the back.

Nothing here is investment, tax, legal, or financial advice. Hartfeld GmbH, Nordmess AG, and the people are invented.

---

## How to be in the room

Read this with a pencil.

Each chapter is one idea from the core finance course, and then the page the case usually does not give you. The order is the order of the course, not the order of a tidy year.

You are sitting where Leonie Voss sits. She has just finished an MBA. On Monday she starts at Hartfeld, her first real job in the firm's finance office. Martin Hartfeld, the CFO, is also the founder's son. He will ask her the question a cold call asks. So will this book.

When you reach a line that says **Write this down**, stop. Write the number, or the yes or no, before you read on. The check is at the end of the chapter, not at the back of the book, so the argument is still in your head when you find out.

Three former classmates keep turning up in her notes.

- Jonas did a summer in banking. His answer is a transaction.
- Amina did a summer in consulting. Her answer is a framework.
- Piotr ran a small plant before the MBA. His answer is what breaks on a Tuesday.

All three are often plausible. One of them is usually wrong in a way that matters.

The last pages of the book are the blue pages: twelve relations, one each, short enough to copy and keep in the folder you actually take to work. Those are the pages to photograph. The chapters are how you earn them.

## The firm, on one page

Hartfeld GmbH makes measuring tools for workshops. It is a private company in Munich. The year just closed is the year ended 31 December 2026.

Klaus Hartfeld, the founder, owns 60 percent. Martin owns 20 percent. Ingrid, Martin's aunt, owns 20 percent. There is no public share price. When this book needs one, it says so, and it builds it.

Sabine Keller is the relationship manager at the house bank. She has known Klaus for twenty years. That fact is not in the spreadsheet. It is in several of the decisions.

## The twelve

1. A later euro is worth less.
2. Risk has a price.
3. Debt is a promise with a fence around it.
4. Cash can leave as a dividend or as a purchase of shares.
5. A price is already a piece of news.
6. A firm can have two values before lunch.
7. Profit does not pay the supplier.
8. The number is made.
9. A forecast is a promise with a date.
10. Tax leaves on a different calendar.
11. A dollar subsidiary is a second set of books.
12. An option is a choice with a price, not a favour.

If you remember only the blue pages, keep them. If you remember only one sentence, remember this. The course is not wrong. It is the first page. The grade is given before the number exists.
