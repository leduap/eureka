# 5. A price is already a piece of news

Nordmess AG is the listed company people mention when they mention Hartfeld. It makes measuring equipment too, with more factories and a ticker. This week a trade paper says Nordmess has lost a French contract. The shares fall 9 percent before Leonie has finished the article.

Martin is pleased, in a way he will not say at dinner. "If we were buyers, that is cheaper. Is the course telling me to wait for bad headlines?"

No. The course is telling him the opposite.

## The price moved because the news was news

A share price is a meeting place. The people who think Nordmess is worth more and the people who think it is worth less have already shouted at each other, and the last shout is the price. When a French contract is lost, the people who had not counted on that contract change their mind, and the price drops by about the size of the surprise.

The drop is not a sale. It is the market subtracting something it had been adding.

**Write this down.** Nordmess falls 9 percent on the headline. You read the same headline an hour later. Do you have an advantage?

You do not. The hour was enough. By the time a story is a story, it is in the price. The course calls this the efficient market, and then spends a week telling you the ways it is not quite true. For a person sitting in Munich with a trade paper and no special access, it is true enough. You will not beat Nordmess by being a careful reader of the trade paper. Careful readers are the market.

What would be an advantage is dull and rare. A fact you have a right to know and others do not, which inside a firm is called your job and outside a firm is sometimes called a crime. Hartfeld's own order book is that kind of fact about Hartfeld. It is not that kind of fact about Nordmess.

## The index does not get to be clever

There is a second machine in this chapter, and it does not read the trade paper at all.

Suppose a new company joins the index that a pension fund has promised to track. The fund does not ask whether the price is wise. The promise was to own what the index owns. The fund buys. If the index adds the company on the day it lists, the fund buys on that day, at that day's price, with every other fund that made the same promise.

Leonie writes the sentence she wants Martin to see. **An index fund is a buyer who is not allowed to wait.**

That is not a criticism of index funds. It is the specification. The cost of being average is that you pay the price of the day the index changes. The alternative is a manager who waits, charges more, and may still be wrong. Chapter 2 already picked a side for the family's idle money: do not pretend to be that manager. This chapter only adds the bill. You will own the expensive new listing if your fund tracks an index that includes it immediately. Some indexes wait. The waiting is the product. Read which one you bought.

Martin's question about Nordmess is the same machine, run backwards. The price fell because the contract left. Waiting for bad headlines, as a strategy, means you believe the headline overstates the damage and that you are early. You are not early. You are holding a newspaper.

## Three hands

Jonas says: buy Nordmess this afternoon. "Nine percent is a dislocation. Dislocations revert."

Amina says: do nothing, and write down why. If Hartfeld one day buys a company, the price you pay has to be justified by Hartfeld's own savings, not by a red day on a screen.

Piotr says: he would call the salesperson at Nordmess and ask whether the French work is the sort of work Hartfeld could do. The share price is not his lead. A customer with a hole in a supply contract might be.

Jonas is trading a headline. Amina is protecting a future negotiation. Piotr is doing commercial work and calling it finance, which is allowed. None of the three has a reason to think 9 percent was the wrong amount.

The Monday rule. **If you learned it from a paper, the price learned it first.**

## Where the case goes quiet

Prices are not wise about things nobody knows, and they are not wise about things a seller is hiding. They are also jumpy in the hour of the headline and calmer a week later, sometimes. A course that says "you cannot beat the market" and a course that says "the market overreacts for a week" are both taught, often by the same department. The usable rule for Leonie is narrower. She has no edge on Nordmess. She has an edge on whether Hartfeld's calibration line earns €690,000 a year, and even that edge expires the day the customers do something else.

The other silence is Hartfeld itself. There is no ticker. Klaus cannot look up whether the family is wrong. Chapter 6 is the substitute, and it will disagree with itself, on purpose.

## Check

1. A 9 percent fall on a public lost contract is the market changing its mind, not a coupon.
2. Reading the same article later is not an advantage.
3. A fund that tracks an index buys a new member because it promised to, at the price of that day.

## Exhibit 5. What the headline already did

| | Before you finished the article |
| --- | --- |
| The fact | Nordmess lost a French contract |
| Who knew | anyone with the trade paper, and the market before them |
| The price | down 9 percent |
| Your edge an hour later | none |
| A useful next act | ask whether Hartfeld can do the work the contract left behind |

The 9 percent is not a number you check. It is a number you refuse to trade on. The useful question is commercial. A customer in France may now be short of tools. That is Piotr's phone call, and it does not require you to own Nordmess.

## The index, in four lines

1. A fund promises to own what the index owns.
2. The index adds a new company.
3. The fund buys, that day, at that price, with every other fund that made the same promise.
4. Waiting for a better price would break the promise. The price of the promise is sometimes a high one.

**Write this down.** Your own savings, if they sit in a fund that tracks an index on the day of entry, will buy that new company. Is that a reason to leave the fund?

Not by itself. It is a reason to know which index you bought, and whether it waits before a new listing goes in. The cost of being average includes this bill. The cost of not being average is a manager, a fee, and a fresh chance to be wrong. Chapter 2 already chose, for money the family is not using in the firm. This chapter only tells you to read the rule you already accepted.

## In the room

The question is the market-efficiency question. What is already in the price? The page the case leaves out is the buyer who is obliged to pay it.
