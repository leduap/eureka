# 8. The number is made

It is the last week of the year. A workshop chain, a smaller one than July's, wants €900,000 of ordinary tools delivered on 28 December. The goods are on the shelf. They cost €540,000 to make. The customer will pay in February, which is normal. The salesperson wants the sale in this year. "The tools will be on their truck before New Year's Eve. That is a sale."

Martin looks at the profit. He is not a villain in this chapter. He would like the year to look like the year he promised Klaus in January. The promise was a photograph. December is where photographs are touched up.

## Book it, and book the dent

Delivered before midnight, with the customer obliged to pay, this is a sale of this year. Revenue €900,000. Cost €540,000. Gross profit **€360,000**. Cash in December: **zero**. Inventory falls. Customers-not-yet-paid rises. The profit is real in the way accountants mean real, and absent in the way Sabine means real.

Hartfeld's tools come back. Not often, but often enough that a silent year is a lie. Leonie uses 4 percent of the sale, because that is what the last three years of returns and repairs cost, and because a round number the firm will actually apply is better than a precise number it will forget. Four percent of €900,000 is **€36,000**.

Gross profit after that provision is **€324,000**. Still no cash.

**Write this down.** If the salesperson's version is booked, with no provision, by how many euros is this year's gross profit flattered?

**€36,000.** Small next to €16,000,000 of gross profit. Large next to a bonus, or a promise, that was missed by €20,000. The flattery is not in the truck. The flattery is in leaving out a cost you already know how to expect.

The provision is not cash either. It is a debt to the future, a guess with a name. Guessing is allowed. Guessing zero, when the last three years were not zero, is a choice.

## Who is paid on the photograph

The course spends this week on the statements as an input to every other week. The discount rate, the multiple, the covenant, the payout: all of them eat this profit. If the profit is a hope with the returns left out, chapter 6 valued a hope, and chapter 3 measured a fence against a hope.

**Your number.** Sabine asks one question in January. "How much of last year's profit had turned into cash by the morning of the board meeting?" You do not have a full cash-flow statement in this scene. What can you say, honestly, about this one sale?

You can say the €324,000 is a claim on a customer and a reserve for repairs, and that on the morning of a January meeting the cash from this truck has not arrived. If the meeting is early enough in February, it still has not arrived. Profit is a statement about the year. Cash is a statement about the day.

## Three hands

Jonas says: book the €900,000, skip the provision, and "clean it up next year if the returns actually happen." Moving a cost into a year where nobody is watching is an old trick. It is also how the next year's photograph starts already dirty.

Amina says: book the sale and the €36,000, and show both lines to Klaus, with the February cash date written beside them. The year can have the profit. The reader of the year has to see the timing.

Piotr says: he wants the salesperson's commission to be paid when the customer pays, not when the truck leaves. "You are arguing about the accounts. The accounts are behaving like the pay packet you wrote."

Piotr has the incentive. Amina has the entry. Jonas has a restatement waiting to happen.

The Monday rule. **If someone is paid when the number is booked, book the number as if the person you trust most were checking it.**

## Where the case goes quiet

Four percent is a policy, not a measurement from this customer. A new chain might return nothing, or might return everything. The case should say whose job it is to look at returns in March and change the 4 percent if it was a fairy tale. If nobody has that job, the provision is decoration.

The other silence is the audit, if there is one, and the tax office, which has its own view of when a sale is a sale. Chapter 10. A profit you book in December can be taxed before the customer pays. The touch-up has a bill.

## Check

1. The December sale can be this year's revenue. Cash in December is zero.
2. Gross profit before the provision is €360,000. The 4 percent provision is €36,000. Profit left is €324,000.
3. Leaving the provision out flatters gross profit by €36,000.
4. A covenant, a multiple, and a value built on this profit inherit the flattery.

## Exhibit 8. The truck, two ways

| | The salesperson's year | The year with the dent booked |

| Revenue in December | 900,000 | 900,000 |
| Cost of the tools | 540,000 | 540,000 |
| Provision for returns and repairs, 4% | 0 | 36,000 |
| Gross profit | 360,000 | 324,000 |
| Cash in December | 0 | 0 |
| Cash when the customer pays | February, if they pay | February, if they pay |

Four percent is the policy because the last three years cost about that much. It is not a measurement of this chain. Write "policy" next to it. In March, someone has to look at what this chain actually returned and change the 4 percent if it was a fairy tale. If that someone does not exist, the provision is a paragraph.

**Write this down.** The year's gross profit, without this truck, is €16,000,000. What share of the year is the €36,000 of flattery?

€36,000 / €16,000,000 is **0.225 percent**. Tiny, and still enough to turn a missed promise into a kept one if the promise was missed by less than that. Size is not the test. The test is whether the photograph is what you say it is.

## Where the flattery travels

Chapter 6 divided cash by a rate and multiplied operating profit by eight. Both of those eat profit. A profit with the returns left out is a larger value and a calmer covenant. Sabine, if she asks for the cash collected by the morning of the board, will not be fooled by the truck. A bonus paid on booked profit will. Ask, before the year is closed, who is paid on which page.

## In the room

The question is the question behind every statement. What was estimated, and who benefits if the estimate is kind? The page the case leaves out is the pay packet tied to the photograph.
