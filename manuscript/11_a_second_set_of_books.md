# 11. A dollar subsidiary is a second set of books

Hartfeld sells mostly in euros. A sales office in Ohio has started selling in dollars, and someone has to put those dollars into a Munich report. The office expects to invoice **$4,000,000** over the year. Nobody is sure whether the dollars will be brought home.

On the day the plan is written, a euro buys, in the teaching rate of this chapter, 1.08 dollars. The invoices are worth

$4,000,000 / 1.08 = **€3,703,704**.

By the time the money could be converted, the euro buys 1.14 dollars. The same invoices are worth

$4,000,000 / 1.14 = **€3,508,772**.

The difference is **€194,932**, and it is not a sales problem. The tools did not get worse. The ruler did.

**Write this down.** Is that €194,932 a loss Hartfeld has suffered, or a translation that has not yet met a bank?

It depends on a fact the case usually hides in a footnote. If Munich is owed the dollars, and will exchange them, the loss is cash, or will be. If the Ohio office is owed the dollars, and will keep them, spend them on American wages, and never send them home, the euro value of the office has shrunk and the wages have become cheaper in euros at the same time. Wealth moved. It did not necessarily move through the Munich bank account this year.

The course has names for these. One is a transaction: a specific pile of foreign money you are going to convert. The other is a translation: the restatement of a whole set of foreign books into the home currency. The names matter less than the question. **Which dollars are going to be exchanged, and when?**

## The hedge is a price, not a personality

The bank will sell Hartfeld the right to convert the $4,000,000 at 1.09 dollars per euro, if the dollars are a real future receipt. At 1.09 the euros are

$4,000,000 / 1.09 = **€3,669,725**.

Compared with the 1.08 in the plan, the hedge gives up **€33,979**. Compared with the 1.14 the world then produced, the hedge saves a great deal. You do not know the 1.14 when you buy the hedge. That is why it has a price. You are paying €33,979 to stop caring.

**Your number.** Klaus says he does not hedge, because hedging is speculation. What is the speculative position: hedging the $4,000,000 you are going to receive, or leaving them unhedged?

Leaving them unhedged is the speculation. The firm has already done the commercial thing. It has promised tools for dollars. Doing nothing is a bet that the dollar will be kind between the promise and the conversion. The hedge is how you stop having an opinion. Klaus's sentence has the word speculation pointing the wrong way. It is a common sentence. Correct it gently. He has been running a good firm.

If the dollars will be spent in Ohio on Ohio wages, a hedge that converts them to euros can be the speculation. You hedged a receipt you were never going to bring home, and you may now have euros when the wages want dollars. Match the hedge to the currency of the bill you actually have to pay.

## Three hands

Jonas says: hedge half. "It shows the board you are balanced."

Amina says: hedge the portion that is invoiced and coming home within the year, and leave the rest until there is an invoice. Do not hedge a salesperson's target.

Piotr says: tell Ohio to price the big contracts in euros, or to put a clause that shares a large move. "You are treating a commercial choice as a banking product."

Amina has the amount. Piotr has the better fix, when the customer will accept it. Jonas has a fraction with no owner. Half of a guess is not a policy.

The Monday rule. **Hedge the money you are going to exchange. Do not hedge a story, and do not call the naked receipt a conservative position.**

## Where the case goes quiet

The rates 1.08, 1.09 and 1.14 are teaching rates. A real forward price includes interest rates in both currencies, which is why the forward is not the same as today's rate even before any opinion about the future. The €33,979 in this chapter is the whole of the gap the case states. In life, part of a forward discount is interest, not fear. If you hedge often, learn that split. If you hedge once a year, the split will not save you from hedging the wrong pile.

The other silence is the accounting line. A transaction loss turns up in profit. A translation of a subsidiary often turns up in equity, under the rules the firm has chosen, and never passes through the profit people use for bonuses. Chapter 8 again. A loss that misses the bonus is still a loss of wealth. Ask which page Sabine reads.

## Check

1. $4,000,000 is €3,703,704 at 1.08 dollars per euro, and €3,508,772 at 1.14. The gap is €194,932.
2. That gap is cash if the dollars will be exchanged. It is a restatement if they will be spent in Ohio.
3. A hedge at 1.09 locks in €3,669,725 and costs €33,979 against the original plan rate.
4. The open position is the speculative one, when a conversion is coming.

## Exhibit 11. The same dollars, three rulers

| Dollars per euro | What $4,000,000 is worth | Against the plan |
| ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 1.08, the plan | 3,703,704 | — |
| 1.09, the hedge | 3,669,725 | costs 33,979 |
| 1.14, the later rate | 3,508,772 | costs 194,932 |

Divide dollars by the number of dollars a euro buys. Do not multiply. A higher number of dollars per euro means a cheaper dollar and fewer euros. The direction is where people lose the plot in the meeting. Say it once, slowly.

**Write this down.** Ohio will spend the whole $4,000,000 on wages in Ohio. Munich hedges it into euros at 1.09 anyway. What have you done?

You have sold dollars you needed. The wages still want dollars. You now have a euro receipt and a dollar bill, which is a new bet, created by the thing Klaus thought was caution. Hedge the pile you will exchange. A target, and a wage bill in the same currency as the sales, are not that pile.

## Two questions, in this order

1. Which dollars will be converted into euros, and in which month?
2. Only then: what is the forward price of that month?

If you cannot answer the first, you are not ready for the second. The bank will sell you the second anyway. That is not the same as it being your position.

## In the room

The question is the currency question. Which unit is the wealth in? The page the case leaves out is whether the money is coming home.
