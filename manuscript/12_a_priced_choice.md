# 12. An option is a choice with a price, not a favour

The chain from July is back. They will take the €6,000,000 run. They would also like, at no extra charge, the right to double it six months from now, at the same prices, if their own shelves are empty.

The salesperson calls this a relationship. The course calls it an option. Both are correct. Only one of them has a number.

If the chain doubles in a tight market, Hartfeld gives up the higher prices it could have charged, and the second run is worth €400,000 less than a free negotiation would have been. That €400,000 is the cost of having promised. If the market is slack, the chain will not double. A right, unlike a duty, is thrown away when it is useless. The payoff of the promise, to Hartfeld, is a loss of €400,000 in the good market and nothing in the bad one. The chain's picture is the mirror. Their gain is Hartfeld's loss. Somebody pays.

## A coin you are not allowed to call fair

The course prices a right by borrowing a simpler thing that is already priced. Here the simpler thing is worth €100 today. In six months it is worth either €130 or €80. The safe rate is 5 percent a year, so money grows 2.5 percent in half a year. €100 becomes €102.50 if it is safe.

Ask what chance of the €130, in a world that only cares about that safe growth, makes the simpler thing fair.

130 × p + 80 × (1 − p) = 102.50

50p = 22.50

p = **0.45**

This 0.45 is not a forecast. It is the weight that makes the arithmetic match the safe rate. Use it on the promise.

Value of the promise today = (0.45 × €400,000 + 0.55 × €0) / 1.025

= €180,000 / 1.025

= **€175,610**

**Write this down.** The gross profit on the original run was €2,400,000. What fraction of that profit have you handed over by adding the right for free?

€175,610 / €2,400,000 is **7.3 percent**. A 7 percent discount that does not appear on the quote.

You can charge for it, cap it, or refuse it. You cannot call it free without deciding, quietly, to cut the price.

**Your number.** The salesperson says the chance they double is only one in ten, so the promise is worth a tenth of €400,000, which is €40,000, and "we can live with that." What has he confused?

He has confused his forecast with the weight the pricing uses, and he has forgotten to discount, which is the smaller error. The larger error is that the one-in-ten is his opinion, and the €400,000 only happens when tools are scarce, which is when a one-in-ten story is most likely to be wrong. The 0.45 is not kinder. It is disciplined. If he wants to use one in ten, he still has to say so as a judgment, and he still has to put €40,000 on the quote. Even his own number is not zero.

## Three hands

Jonas says: charge €175,000 for the right, or do not give it. "This is the whole chapter. Do not decorate it."

Amina says: give a right to an extra €2,000,000, not an extra €6,000,000. Price that smaller right the same way, and call it a service level.

Piotr says: he will not promise the second run at today's prices unless the parts for it are parts he can sell elsewhere. "You have priced the margin. You have not priced the scrap if they double and then argue."

Jonas has the clean answer. Amina has the negotiable one. Piotr has the way the option becomes a worse option than the model, because the model assumed the only bad news was a slack market in which the chain walks away. A double order of unsaleable tools, followed by a dispute, is a payoff the model set at zero and life did not.

The Monday rule. **A right you give away is a discount. Put the discount on the page, or do not give the right.**

## Where the case goes quiet

The €400,000 in the tight market is a judgment, like the beta on the smart gauge. Halve it and the right is worth €87,805, which is still not a favour. The decision to write it down survives the judgment. The price on the quote does not. Show the range.

This chapter is not a chapter about selling options every month in a brokerage account, for income, against shares you own or cash you have set aside. That is a different activity. It has its own risks, its own tax, and its own books. Nothing in Hartfeld's promise should be used to justify it, and nothing in those books should be used to price a customer's right to double an order. The two subjects share a word. They do not share a decision.

One more silence, and then the blue pages. The course will let you leave believing you can price any choice, once you have a tree with two branches. Most choices in this firm do not have a traded twin worth €100. When there is no twin, say "judgment" and write the number anyway. A number with a label beats a relationship with no number.

## Check

1. The weight that matches the safe rate is 0.45, not a forecast.
2. The right is worth €175,610 today.
3. That is 7.3 percent of the €2,400,000 gross profit on the run.
4. "They will probably not use it" is not the same sentence as "it is worth nothing."

## Exhibit 12. The right, on a postcard

A simpler thing is worth €100 today, €130 in the good half-year, €80 in the bad. Safe money grows 2.5 percent in six months, so €100 of it becomes €102.50.

130p + 80(1 − p) = 102.50, so p = **0.45**. That weight is not a forecast.

The promise costs Hartfeld €400,000 in the good market and nothing in the bad one, because a right is dropped when it is useless.

| | |
| --- | ---: |
| Weighted payoff | 0.45 × 400,000 = 180,000 |
| Brought back half a year | 180,000 / 1.025 |
| Value of the right | 175,610 |
| Gross profit on the original run | 2,400,000 |
| The quiet discount | 7.3 percent |

**Write this down.** Halve the pain in the good market, to €200,000. What is the right worth, and do you still refuse to call it free?

Half the pain, same weight, same discounting: **€87,805**. Still a number. Still a line on the quote. The judgment moves the price. It does not move the zero.

## What this chapter is not

It is not a method for selling options every month against shares, or against cash set aside to buy shares, in order to manufacture income. That activity has its own losses, its own tax, and its own books. Hartfeld's customer is not that activity. If you remember one separation from this book, remember that one. A shared word is not a shared decision.

## In the room

The question is the option question, in the only form most operating firms ever meet it. What did we promise, and what would we charge a stranger for that promise? The page the case leaves out is the scrap, and the temptation to treat a customer's favour as if it had no price.
