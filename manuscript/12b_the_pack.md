# The pack

The chapters were one idea at a time, because that is how a course is examined. Hartfeld does not have that luxury. On a Thursday in the late autumn Leonie puts twelve pages on the table, in the order of the course, and asks the room to decide only what still has a decision left in it. Klaus is at the head. Martin is on his right. Ingrid has come because page four is about her house, and she has said she will not be discussed in her absence. Sabine has been invited for the pages that spend her money, and she has the good manners to sit slightly back, which does not mean she is slightly involved.

Leonie says, before anyone picks up a page, that the figures are the teaching figures. Thirty percent, so a person can check them. Munich is 32.975 percent, and the gap on the year's profit is €166,600, and she will say so when a decision is close. Nothing in the pack is a promise that next year will look like last year. Last year is the photograph they have.

**Write this down.** Which three pages actually need a yes or a no today, and which pages are only there so that a yes on one of them does not quietly ruin another?

## Page one, the line, which has already been bought

The calibration line is not a prospective purchase anymore. The minute from January said yes, at €259,145 even on the family's 8 percent, and at €430,708 on the 6 percent the rate chapter built. Cash was €690,000 a year. The bank balance, before any customer paid, was €300,000 overdrawn. That overdraft was named, and Sabine covered it, and the customers were slower than sixty days and faster than a disaster. The page stays in the pack because October has to be able to see what January believed.

October's revenue is €900,000, not €1,500,000. Cash from the line is €270,000 a year if the rest of the firm can use the tax loss. Known in January, that would have been a no: −€1,338,484 at 6 percent. The €2,400,000 cheque does not get a vote now. A dealer at €1,500,000, plus the parts at cost, puts €1,800,000 in the bank. Keeping is worth €1,361,516. Stopping is ahead by €438,484.

Klaus looks at the €438,484 as if it were an accusation. Leonie does not let it be one. January used the forecast January had. October uses the offer October has. If the dealer will only pay €900,000 and will not take the parts, they keep the line, because keeping is still worth €1,361,516 and the parts are worthless off the machine. The answer follows the offer. Pride does not get a column.

Martin wants the word "mistake" kept out of the minute. He can have that. The minute can say "new decision." What he cannot have is January's net present value copied forward as if the revenue had not moved. A forecast has a date. The date is in the room.

Piotr, who is not in the room and is in her notes, would ask whether the missing €600,000 is a late customer or a product. She says she does not know, and she has written that she does not know, which is more useful than a confident story. Sabine asks only whether the line is inside the fence if they keep it. It is a small line. The fence, if it breaks, will break for a larger reason. They park the dealer at €1,500,000 as the decision, and Leonie is told to get the offer in writing before anyone mourns the machine. A spoken €1,500,000 is not a cheque.

## Page two, the gauge, which should not borrow the line's rate

The engineer is still hopeful. The gauge costs €1,800,000 and ties up €500,000. Cash coming back is €500,000 a year, rebuilt from €2,000,000 of sales, €1,440,000 of cash cost, €360,000 of depreciation and €200,000 of operating profit. At the firm's 6 percent the net present value is €179,811. At 12 percent it is −€213,898.

Leonie does not ask the room to fall in love with 12 percent. She asks them to notice that the yes and the no are the same project. The firm's rate was built for a caliper: safe euro 3 percent, market extra 5 percent, beta 0.90, equity cost 7.5 percent, debt 3.5 percent after tax because there is profit, book weights because there is no share price, blend 5.99 percent, called 6. A beta of 1.20, if Klaus wants the firm to sound riskier, still only produces 6.92 percent. His old hurdle of 8 percent is harsher than the method. It is allowed to be. It is not allowed to be pasted onto a subscription product and also onto a caliper and called consistency.

She has written 1.60 next to the gauge and labelled it a judgment. There is no regression. Nordmess's beta, if someone brings one, is a clue, not a measurement of this family. The gauge does not clear a harsh reading. They do not do it. Jonas would say the board cannot bear two hurdles. The pack has two hurdles on one page, with the judgments in ink, precisely so the board can bear them. A single rate would have approved the exciting project because the exciting project is the one the engineer loves.

Klaus asks whether refusing the gauge makes him a man who refuses the future. Leonie says it makes him a man who refuses a future he has not priced. If the engineer can show cash that survives a rate near 8.2 percent, which is the ledger's blend at a beta of 1.60, the page can be reopened. Hope is not a cash flow. They leave the gauge in the drawer. The drawer is not an insult. It is a place a page can wait without spending money.

## Page three, the loan, sized to a year Klaus remembers

Sabine has not pushed. The money is there: another €6,000,000 at 5.5 percent. The old €8,000,000 stays at 5 percent. Interest together is €730,000, not a blended fairy tale in which the whole €14,000,000 reprices. In a year like the one just closed, net debt over EBITDA is 1.61 and cover is 8.2. The fence holds. In a year of €4,000,000 EBITDA and €2,800,000 of operating profit, the ratio is 2.90 and the cover is 3.84. The fence was 2.0 and 4.0. Both lines break.

She does not add €2,400,000 of tax shield to a value already computed at 6 percent. The shield is in the 6 percent. Adding it again counts Sabine twice. The permanent-debt story, value with no debt about €68,200,000 plus the shield, about €70,600,000, is a different machine, and she says the word permanent. A ten-year loan that pays down is a smaller shield than 30 percent of the opening balance.

Amina's backward sum is the one she puts in front of Klaus. Bad-year operating profit of €2,800,000, cover of 4, interest allowed €700,000, old loan already €400,000, room for new interest €300,000, new debt at 5.5 percent **€5,454,545**, and only if cash does not shrink. The offer is past the room. If cash also falls €1,000,000, net debt on the big loan is €12,600,000 and the ratio is worse than 2.90. She asks Klaus, not the spreadsheet, whether the €4,000,000 year is a fantasy. He is quiet in the way of a man who has had the phone call. They do not sign €6,000,000. They ask Sabine for a smaller line, inside the bad year's cover, and they ask her, on the back page, whether anyone's house is in the sentence. A guarantee is not in the theorem. It is sometimes on the term sheet. She would like to know before Thursday becomes a signature.

Sabine says she would rather lend a number the bad year can carry than collect a breach from a friend. This is the relationship doing exactly the work the course cannot mark and the firm cannot do without. Leonie writes it down as Sabine's sentence, not as a reason to ignore the ratio.

## Page four, Ingrid, and the cash the line already used

Ingrid's house is not a metaphor. She wants €1,200,000 out. Profit after tax was €3,920,000, so the request is about 31 percent of the profit, and it is half the cash. Her 20 percent of a dividend would be €240,000 gross. At the lecture's 26.375 percent she would keep €176,700, and Leonie says the tax is in pencil, because a fifth of a GmbH is often not in that box. The adviser can rub the pencil out. Nobody in this room will replace the adviser.

Martin says the course prefers a buyback. Leonie asks which course. On the imaginary million shares at €42.40, a €1,200,000 purchase retires about 28,302 shares. If Ingrid is the only seller, her stake falls from 20 percent to about 17.7 percent. If everyone sells in proportion, she still owns 20 percent and her cash matches the dividend, and Klaus and Martin have to take cash too. The pie does not grow. The family as a whole holds less than the firm gave up, because the tax office keeps a slice. The knife is not the question. The question is whether she wants to be smaller in the firm, and whether the cash is free.

It is not free. The line and its parts wanted €2,700,000 before customers paid. A full payout would have left €1,200,000 and a hole of €1,500,000. Even a phased €400,000 leaves a hole of €700,000. The line has now been bought, and October may sell it, which would put cash back. Until the dealer pays, the cash is not back. They offer Ingrid €400,000 now, against the roof, and a date for the rest that sits after the dealer has either paid or been refused, and after Sabine has seen the page. Ingrid is angry in a specific way, which is better than a range. She asks whether her 20 percent is being spent on Martin's minute. Leonie says the minute is a record, not a claim on the aunt's house. The calendar is the claim. A roof can be phased. She does not pretend the phasing is painless.

They do not settle an exit. €42.40 is a ruler from the next pages, not a price. A notary sits between a German private company and a casual buyback, and creditors, including the woman sitting slightly back from the table, rely on capital that a lecture does not mention. The lawyer is not in the room. The decision not to improvise a buyback on a Thursday is the decision that respects that fact.

## Page five, the newspaper, which they will not trade

Nordmess fell 9 percent on a French contract, in a week that is already over. Martin no longer looks pleased. The pack says the fall was the market subtracting a contract, not a coupon. An hour later there was no edge. There is still no edge. They do not buy the shares. If a salesperson wants to call the workshop that lost a supplier, that is a sales call, and it does not need a brokerage account attached to it.

Klaus asks, because he always asks, whether the family's savings outside the firm should be cleverer than an index. The pack repeats the choice already made. An index fund buys what the index adds, on the day it is added, because it promised to. That bill is the cost of being average. The cost of not being average is a manager, a fee, and a fresh chance to be wrong. They do not reopen the choice because of one red day. They do check which index they own, and whether it waits before a new listing goes in. Reading the rule you already accepted is not a trade.

## Page six, two values, and no average

Klaus asks again for one number, because Ingrid is in the room and a phased cheque is not the same as knowing what she owns. Leonie gives him the sentence.

Steady cash, no growth, machines replaced and no extra inventory, is €4,200,000 a year. At 6 percent the business is €70,000,000. Net debt €5,600,000 comes off. Equity is €64,400,000. Her 20 percent is €12,880,000. Eight times operating profit is a business of €48,000,000 and equity of €42,400,000. Her 20 percent is €8,480,000. The gap is an 8.75 percent buyer, or a buyer who does not believe the cash lasts. They do not say €59,000,000. Nordmess, for scale and not for flattery, is a different firm: €50,000,000 of operating profit in the teaching rumour, eight times that is €400,000,000, net debt €40,000,000, equity €360,000,000. A multiple borrows the kind of firm. It does not borrow the size, and it does not turn Hartfeld into a listed company.

A buyer who cannot sell on Monday will try to pay less. At 25 percent off the multiple, her fifth is €6,360,000. That discount is a negotiation, not a measurement. It moves if the buyer is given a way out, a seat, or a dividend that is not a surprise. Ingrid looks at €12,880,000 and then at €6,360,000 and says she can see who the pages are for. Leonie says they are for stopping a Sunday in five years at which someone feels robbed. The distance is the argument. Collapsing it is how the robbery is arranged in advance.

They do not set a sale price. They set the two numbers any sale is allowed to argue from. The photograph is last year. If last year was flattered, both numbers inherited it. Which is the next page but one.

## Page seven, the order, which is a loan with a factory in the middle

The July order is still the shape of the thing they get offered. €6,000,000 of tools, cost €3,600,000, gross profit €2,400,000, forty percent, ninety days of cash missing after they pay the supplier. Interest €44,384. Profit after the carry €2,355,616, before overhead and tax. The margin survives. The cash on the balance sheet, €2,400,000, does not cover €3,600,000. Borrowing the whole amount takes net debt to €9,200,000, about 1.3 times EBITDA of €7,200,000, inside a fence of 2.0, until a second order stacks on the first.

They will not give up €80,000 of price to save €14,795 of interest. They will ask whether Klaus's limit on one customer is a rule or a story. He says, today, that it is a rule at €2,000,000, and that he has waived it before for names he trusted, and that he does not want to waive it for a special specification. Special tools are scrap if the chain walks. The interest is not the risk. The scrap is. They will take a smaller order, or a standard one, and they will tell Sabine before they tell the customer. Ordinary cash is already out for 73 days: 46 of customers, 73 of inventory, 46 of suppliers. Stretching the habit is a decision. It will look like one in the minute.

The tax dates sit on the same page, because Piotr asked for them and he was right. Trade tax on 15 February, 15 May, 15 August and 15 November. Corporate tax on 10 March, 10 June, 10 September and 10 December. A customer on ninety days does not negotiate with those dates. A quarterly slice of the teaching tax is €420,000, and Munich's slice is larger. February is a month, not a footnote.

## Page eight, the truck, which is how a year gets flattered

The December question has not arrived yet, and Leonie puts it in the pack anyway, because the valuation they just refused to average eats whatever profit the year ends with. A truck on 28 December can be this year's revenue. €900,000 of sales, €540,000 of cost, €360,000 of gross profit, cash in December zero. A 4 percent provision for the returns the last three years actually produced is €36,000. Profit left is €324,000. Skipping the provision flatters the year by 0.225 percent of gross profit. Tiny, and enough to turn a missed promise into a kept one.

She asks who is paid when the number is booked. Martin says the sales commission is paid on delivery, and that changing it will be a fight. She says the fight is the point. If the commission stays on the truck, the provision matters more, not less. Four percent is a policy. Someone is named to look at the actual returns in March. If that someone is nobody, the provision is decoration. Klaus names Martin, which is slightly cruel and slightly correct. The person who wants the photograph should be the person who looks at it in March.

## Page nine is page one, seen from October

She does not make them do the sunk cost twice. She points at the dealer and at the original minute and says the minute stays in the file as a record. The pack's decision is the offer. The technicians are counted once. If their wages end with the line, they are inside the €720,000 that stopping escapes. If the wages continue, they are a cost of the firm either way, and the dealer's offer looks better. The tax saving inside the €270,000 needs the rest of the firm to have profit. If the whole firm is in the hole, keeping is worth less than €1,361,516. She has written the condition on the page. A condition that stays in her head is not a condition the minute will remember.

## Page ten, the city, without rebuilding every sum

They are not close on the line's original yes, and she does not rebuild it. Yearly cash at Munich's stack is €681,075 against €690,000. The yes stood. She says this so that nobody throws out the year's work because 30 percent was a classroom rate, and so that nobody signs the next close call at 30 percent and calls it international. Depreciation of €1,200,000 shields €360,000 a year at the working rate and requires no cheque. Interest does not shield trade tax in the simple way the debt chapter used for teaching. If a later loan is being justified by the shield, an adviser sees the add-back before a signature. The pack names the adviser as a step, not as a person in the room, because the person is not here and inventing their opinion would be a different kind of flattery.

## Page eleven, the dollars, which are a question before they are a rate

Ohio expects to invoice $4,000,000. At 1.08 dollars per euro that is €3,703,704. At 1.14 it is €3,508,772. The gap of €194,932 is cash if the dollars will be exchanged, and a restatement if they will be spent on Ohio wages. Nobody at the table is sure which. Leonie refuses the forward until someone is sure. A hedge at 1.09 would lock in €3,669,725 and cost €33,979 against the plan. The open receipt, if the money is coming home, is the bet. Hedging dollars that wages already need would open a bet and call it caution. Klaus's sentence, that hedging is speculation, is pointed the wrong way when a conversion is coming, and pointed the right way when it is not. The pack's decision is to answer the first question in writing, from Ohio, before Sabine is asked for a price. The bank will sell the price anyway. That is not the same as it being their position.

The rates are teaching rates. A real forward includes interest in both currencies. She says so, and she does not let the sophistication choose the pile.

## Page twelve, the right they will not give away

The chain wants, for nothing, the right to double a run six months from now at today's prices. In the tight market that costs Hartfeld €400,000 against a free negotiation. In the slack market the chain will not use the right. The weight that matches a safe 5 percent, on the simpler thing worth €100 today and €130 or €80 in six months, is 0.45. It is not a forecast. The right is worth €175,610, which is 7.3 percent of the €2,400,000 gross profit on the original run. Halve the pain and it is still €87,805. The salesperson's one-in-ten is his forecast. Undiscounted, a tenth of €400,000 is €40,000, which is still not a blank line.

They will charge, cap, or refuse. They will not call it a relationship and leave it off the quote. If the parts for a doubled run cannot be sold elsewhere, Piotr's scrap sits on top of the postcard, and the postcard is only a lower bound. This page is not a page about selling options for income against shares. Leonie says that out loud, because the word is about to travel, and a shared word is not a shared decision. Klaus, who does not sell options and does not intend to start, looks relieved and slightly confused, which is the correct response to a distinction you are glad exists.

## What the afternoon actually decided

She reads the decisions back, slowly, because a pack that is not read back becomes twelve private memories.

The gauge is a no until its own cash survives a rate that belongs to it. The extra loan is not €6,000,000. It is sized to the bad year, and the offer is past €5,454,545 of room. Ingrid gets €400,000 now and a date, not an exit and not a lecture about buybacks. Nordmess is not a purchase. The firm is worth two numbers, €64,400,000 and €42,400,000 of equity, and her fifth is €12,880,000 or €8,480,000 or €6,360,000 after a negotiable discount, and nobody averages them. The special order is smaller, or standard, and Sabine hears first. The December truck, when it comes, is booked with the €36,000 dent, and the commission is a fight they are going to have. The line is sold if the dealer will pay €1,500,000 and the parts come back at cost, and kept if the offer falls to €900,000 and the parts are trapped. The dollars are not hedged until Ohio says whether they are coming home. The right to double is not free.

Klaus asks whether this is what the course wanted. She says the course wanted the arithmetic, and the arithmetic is in the pages. The afternoon wanted the arithmetic not to be spent twice, and not to be braver than the month with the lowest bank balance. The grade, she does not say, was given when they agreed to write the decisions down before the next person made them feel unkind. She does say the blue pages are what she will keep in the folder. The pack is how they earned them, this time, on this firm, with these euros.

## In the room

The question is no longer one question. It is whether the firm can hold twelve true sentences on one afternoon without letting the convenient one spend the cash. The page a course leaves out is the table.
