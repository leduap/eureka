# The blue pages

Copy these. They are the course, after the stories have been taken off.

The teaching rate in the examples was 30 percent. The firm was Hartfeld. Neither fact is required for the twelve lines. A close decision uses the city's tax rate and the project's risk, not the rate that was easy to divide.

## 1. A later euro

Cash is what you discount. Depreciation is an allocation of cash you have already counted.

Net present value is the value of the cash you get, minus the cash you give up.

A positive value is not yet a yes. Name the month the bank balance is lowest, and who covers it.

## 2. A rate

The rate belongs to the project, not to the letterhead.

Cost of equity = safe rate + beta × the extra for owning the market.

After-tax cost of debt = interest rate × (1 − tax rate), and only while there is profit to shield.

Weighted average = equity's weight × its cost + debt's weight × its after-tax cost.

## 3. Debt

Without tax, splitting the same pie does not change the size of the pie.

With tax, debt is a shield. Do not add that shield to a value you already computed at a weighted average that contains it.

Size the debt to the bad year in the covenant, not to the offer.

## 4. Payout

Cash to owners is cash that is no longer in the firm. The pie does not grow because you sliced it.

A dividend and a proportional buyback are the same slice, at a listed price, before tax and before law.

They are not the same paperwork in a private company, and they are not available if the machines need the cash that month.

## 5. A price

By the time you have read the news, the price has read it too.

An index fund buys what the index adds, at the price of that day, because it promised to. That is the specification, not a scandal.

## 6. Two values

Steady cash, divided by the rate, is one value. A peer's multiple is another.

If they disagree, say so. The average is not a third method.

Last year is a photograph. A buyer is not buying the photograph unless you have promised it will continue.

## 7. Days

Cash tied up ≈ cost of the goods, for the days between paying and being paid.

Interest on that gap is usually small. Scrap, if the customer walks, is not.

Days already in the firm = days of customers + days of inventory − days of suppliers.

## 8. A made number

Revenue you book before you are paid is not cash.

A cost you can already expect, and do not book, is flattery.

Anyone paid when the number is booked should be assumed to be in the room when you estimate it.

## 9. A dated forecast

When the date arrives, the old net present value is a record, not an instruction.

The money already spent does not get a vote. The offer for what remains does.

## 10. Tax

A classroom rate makes projects look better than the city will, if the city charges more.

Tax cash has due dates. A customer's terms do not move those dates.

Depreciation lowers the tax without a cheque. Add it back after you have used it, or you will count it twice.

## 11. Currency

A move in the rate is cash if you are going to exchange the money. It is a restatement if you are going to spend it where it is.

The unhedged receipt is the bet. The hedge is the absence of an opinion.

Hedge the pile you will exchange. Do not hedge a target.

## 12. A right

A right the other side will use only when it hurts you has a price.

"They may not use it" is not "it is worth nothing."

Write the price on the quote, or do not give the right.

A monthly option sold for income, in a brokerage account, is not this decision. Do not let the shared word do the thinking.
