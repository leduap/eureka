# Sources and notes

The cases, the firm, and the people are invented. The teaching rates that are not tied to a statute are choices, and the chapters say so. What follows is the short list a reader can check.

## Tax, used in chapter 10

The running examples use 30 percent so the arithmetic can be checked by hand. That is not the Munich bill.

- Corporate income tax at 15 percent: Körperschaftsteuergesetz § 23 (1).
- Solidarity surcharge at 5.5 percent of the corporate tax, including for companies: Solidaritätszuschlaggesetz § 4.
- Trade tax at 3.5 percent of the base, times the municipal multiplier: Gewerbesteuergesetz § 11 (2) and § 16.
- Munich's multiplier, 490 percent, is the rate published by the city and in force for many years, including 2026. The city sets it. Another city does not owe you 490 percent.

15 + (15 × 0.055) + (3.5 × 4.90) = 32.975 percent, before the add-backs and deductions that make a real assessment different from a textbook profit. Interest is not fully deductible for trade tax. The shield in chapter 3 is a teaching shield.

Instalment dates used in the chapter are the ordinary ones: trade tax on 15 February, 15 May, 15 August and 15 November; corporate tax on 10 March, 10 June, 10 September and 10 December. The assessment can move a particular year. The point of the chapter is that a date exists.

Flat tax on private investment income, used as a pencil figure for Ingrid in chapter 4, is 25 percent plus the surcharge, 26.375 percent together. A person who owns 20 percent of a private company is often not in that simple box. The gross dividend is the figure to keep. The tax is for her adviser.

## The finance ideas

The twelve ideas are the common core of an MBA finance course: present value, risk and the cost of capital, capital structure, payout, market prices, valuation, working capital, the statements those calculations eat, forecasts, tax as cash, currency, and options. They are taught from large books, and the large books remain the reference for anyone who wants the proofs.

This book does not replace one of those texts. It is the second page: the same ideas, each one stopped at the place a case usually stops, and then asked what the firm still has to decide.

A few of the classroom forms this book is deliberately not copying, so a reader who has met them will recognise the question and not the furniture:

- a capital-budgeting case with a long-lived asset and a tax depreciation schedule
- a divisional cost-of-capital case
- a debt-policy case in which the firm looks under-borrowed
- a seasonal working-capital case in which profit and the bank loan disagree

Hartfeld's numbers are its own. They are generated by `ledger/ledger.py` in this manuscript. If a figure in the text and a figure in the ledger ever disagree, the ledger is the one to trust, and the text should be corrected.

## Rates that are not facts

The safe rate of 3 percent, the market extra of 5 percent, the betas of 0.90 and 1.60, the peer multiple of eight times, and the exchange rates 1.08, 1.09 and 1.14 are teaching choices. They are labelled where they are used. None of them is a forecast, and none of them is a recommendation to borrow, invest, hedge, or sell a firm.

## What this book refuses to be

It is not an investment programme. It is not a guide to selling options for income. It is not tax advice for a German shareholder, and it is not a commentary on any real company, including the listed firms an index fund will buy because an index told it to.

The grade, in the courses this book is thinking of, is given for the first page: the setup, the discounting, the ratio, the multiple. The job starts on the second page. That is the only claim the book needs.
